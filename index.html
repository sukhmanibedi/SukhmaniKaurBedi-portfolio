Welcome

Enterprise design isn't about building beautiful products. It's about understanding the person who has to use that product at 9am on a Monday, under pressure, with no margin for error — and the business that depends on them getting it right.

Most design problems in complex systems aren't interface problems. They're the result of workflows that were never designed, business logic that was never questioned, and friction that accumulated quietly until users just learned to work around it. Finding that friction — before it reaches the user — is where I spend most of my time.

I'm Sukhmani. At IBM, I design enterprise software for high-stakes client environments: IBM PowerVC, Atradius, and European Central Bank core platforms. Because in this space, empathy alone isn't enough — you have to hold the user's reality and the business's constraints in the same frame, and design the experience that makes both possible.

MY FRAMEWORK

Process

How enterprise problems get solved: the design framework behind the system.

💡

Map the system, not the screen

Every enterprise screen is connected to a larger ecosystem. Before designing an interface, I trace the data flows, dependencies, and business rules that govern it. A screen that looks clean in isolation is useless if it doesn't fit the workflow.

📌

Find where complexity is hiding

Complex systems don't start out complex—they accumulate edge cases over time. My job is to locate the friction points that users have normalized, separate essential complexity from artificial complexity, and design interfaces that make the hard stuff manageable.

⚖️

Design within real constraints

Enterprise UX happens at the intersection of technical feasibility, business logic, and regulatory mandates. I work alongside engineers and domain experts from day one, ensuring designs don't just look good in Figma, but ship reliably in production.

🛡️

Validate before it ships to people who can't opt out

Consumer apps lose users when they fail; enterprise users lose time, make costly errors, or face burnout. I test with actual operators under realistic conditions—stress-testing workflows before they hit production environments.
